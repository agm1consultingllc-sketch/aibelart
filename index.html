The Artist

Abel Garcia Medina was born in Arroyo Arenas, Havana, Cuba, and landed at Miami International Airport with his family on July 30, 1995. He grew up in Hialeah — and Hialeah is still home, still the studio, still the subject.

He came to art in 2022 and hasn't stopped: roughly 2,000 works in four years — paintings, portraits, digital geometry — each one signed, and each one built the same way, from bold shapes, saturated color and a heavy black line that holds it all together.

In 2026 his geometric work placed 10th in his group in The People's Artist, a national art contest. His pieces have also grown past the canvas: his characters Bleepy & Blipon anchor MisterFu Stories, a children's story and coloring project built from his geometric pieces.

The ambition is simple and he says it plainly: make the work big enough, and honest enough, that it outlives him — so his son Jay can point at it one day and say, that was my father.

Next: The Walls

Public art — North Miami

The City of North Miami's Uptown CRA has approved its Public Art Implementation Plan 2026–2029 for the NW 7th Avenue corridor — and Abel's art is in the conversation for its walls.

The plan runs on two themes, Heritage and Innovation, for a corridor envisioned as Miami-Dade County's premier African American and Caribbean cultural corridor. That's Abel's story in two words: a kid from Arroyo Arenas, raised in Hialeah, building a new visual language out of pure geometry.

He scales his work the old-school way — the grid method, transferring a design square by square from paper to wall, the same grid structure the face at left is built on. Murals, commissions and the CRA's pre-qualified artist recruitment are the next step.
